# 6102 — 1K x 1 static RAM

## Function

A memory of 1024 words of one bit each, which can be read and written while the power is on. Its contents are random at power-on and lost when the power goes off. It is MOS Technology's copy of the Intel 2102: eight side by side, one per data line, make 1024 bytes, as on the KIM-1.

`CE_N` low selects the chip. With `RW` high, the bit at the address on `A0`–`A9` appears on `DOUT` at most 500 ns after the address settles or `CE_N` falls. With `RW` low, the chip writes `DIN` into that bit and `DOUT` is off (floating); the address and `DIN` must stay put until `RW` goes back high, and the bit keeps what they held then. With `CE_N` high, `DOUT` is off.

The data goes in on one pin and comes out on another. On the KIM-1, `DIN` sits on the data bus and `DOUT` reaches it through a 74125 buffer that is on only while the processor reads this memory.

The inputs are MOS: a pin left floating keeps reading its last value. The 500 ns access is the KIM-1 parts list's; the chip-enable and output-off times are assumed (the 2102 family's and the 2114's), and so is `DOUT` floating during a write.

## Pins

- `A0`–`A9`: address; `A0` is the lowest bit
- `DIN`: data in
- `DOUT`: data out, off when not reading
- `CE_N`: chip enable, active low
- `RW`: high reads, low writes
- `VCC`: +5 V (pin 10)
- `GND`: 0 V (pin 9)
