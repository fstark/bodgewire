# 6502 — 8-bit microprocessor (NMOS)

## Function

A processor: it reads a program from memory and runs it, one bus cycle per clock cycle. Each cycle starts when `PHI0` falls: the 6502 puts an address on `A0`–`A15` and says on `RW` whether it reads (high) or writes (low). While `PHI0` is high (`PHI2` high), memory puts the byte on `D0`–`D7` for a read, or the 6502 drives it for a write; a read takes the byte when `PHI0` falls. `SYNC` is high during the cycle that fetches an instruction's first byte. `PHI1` and `PHI2` are the clock's two phases, for the chips around it.

- `RES_N` low resets it: on release it reads the address to start from at `FFFC` (low byte) and `FFFD` (high byte). At power-on its registers and its program counter hold whatever they happen to: without a reset it runs from anywhere.
- `IRQ_N` low asks for an interrupt, between two instructions, unless the program has masked it; `NMI_N` going low always gets one. Their routines start at the addresses read at `FFFE`–`FFFF` and `FFFA`–`FFFB`.
- `RDY` low makes it repeat a read cycle, holding the bus still, until it goes high again; it does not stop a write.
- `SO` going low sets the overflow flag.
- Every opcode does something, the undocumented ones too; twelve of them jam the processor until a reset, its address bus stuck at `FFFF`.
- Its inputs are MOS: an input left floating keeps the level it last had.

## Pins

- `A0`–`A9`, `A10`–`A15`: address; `A0` is the lowest bit
- `D0`–`D7`: data, driven by the 6502 only while writing; `D0` is the lowest bit
- `RW`: high while reading, low while writing
- `PHI0`: clock in, 1 MHz
- `PHI1`: clock out, high while `PHI0` is low
- `PHI2`: clock out, follows `PHI0`
- `SYNC`: high while fetching an instruction
- `RDY`: ready; low holds a read cycle
- `IRQ_N`: interrupt request, low to ask
- `NMI_N`: non-maskable interrupt, on its falling edge
- `RES_N`: reset, low to reset
- `SO`: set overflow, on its falling edge
- `NC1`–`NC3`: not connected
- `VCC`: +5 V
- `GND`, `GND2`: 0 V (both)
