# MC3446 — quad IEEE-488 bus transceiver

## Function

Four transceivers between a computer's port pins and the IEEE-488 (GPIB) bus, where printers and disk drives hang on one cable. Each channel has a driver, a bus pin and a receiver:

- the driver pulls its bus pin (`BUSA`–`BUSD`) low while its input (`DA`–`DD`) is low and the channel's enable is low; otherwise it lets go, and the chip's own bus termination pulls the line high. Several chips on one bus line can each pull it low, as open-collector outputs do;
- the receiver output (`RA`–`RD`) copies the bus line, whoever drives it.

Neither direction inverts. `ENABC_N` enables the drivers of channels A, B and C; `END_N` channel D. The PET ties both low: its software releases a line by writing 1.

In this simulation the termination is the bus pin's own weak pull-up, so another chip driving the line high beats the driver's low; the real driver sinks 48 mA and would win. Nothing on the BW-2001 drives the bus but these chips.

Delays (Motorola MC3446A, maximums): driver input to bus 40 ns up, 50 ns down; enable to bus 50 ns; bus to receiver output 50 ns up, 40 ns down.

## Pins

- `DA`–`DD`: driver inputs
- `BUSA`–`BUSD`: bus lines, pulled low by the driver, high by the termination
- `RA`–`RD`: receiver outputs, copying the bus
- `ENABC_N`: enables the drivers of channels A–C, active low
- `END_N`: enables the driver of channel D, active low
- `VCC`: +5 V
- `GND`: 0 V
