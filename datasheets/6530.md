# 6530 — ROM, RAM, I/O and timer (KIM mask 002 or 003)

## Function

Four things in one MOS chip: a 1024-byte ROM, a 64-byte RAM, two 8-bit ports (A and B) and an interval timer. It sits on a 6502's bus and works in step with its `PHI2` clock. The ROM's contents and which addresses select what are fixed when the chip is made, by its mask; the KIM-1 has two, the 6530-002 and the 6530-003, each holding half of its monitor program.

Selecting (both KIM masks): `RS0` low selects the ROM, whose byte at `A0`–`A9` the processor reads. `RS0` high with `CS1` low selects the RAM or the ports and timer, by `A9`–`A6`: on the 002, `1111` is the RAM (`$17C0` on the KIM) and `1101` the ports and timer (`$1740`); on the 003, `1110` the RAM (`$1780`) and `1100` the ports and timer (`$1700`). The RAM uses `A0`–`A5`.

Ports and timer, by the low address bits: with `A2` low, `A1 A0` pick port A's data (`00`), port A's direction (`01`), port B's data (`10`), port B's direction (`11`). A direction bit of 1 makes that pin an output, which drives its data bit: a 0 pulls the pin down hard, a 1 only through a pull-up. A port output's 0 is weaker than a TTL output's high: a gate driving the same line high wins (about 2 mA against tens of mA). A direction bit of 0 makes it an input, held high by the same pull-up until something pulls it down (a key to a scanned row, another chip). Reading a port gives the data bit for each output pin and the pin itself for each input pin.

With `A2` high, writing a number `N` starts the timer counting down once every 1, 8, 64 or 1024 clock pulses (`A1 A0` = `00`, `01`, `10`, `11`). Reading with `A0` low gives the count: `N − 1 − k/P` (rounded down) `k` pulses after the write, for a rate of one every `P`. After `N × P` pulses the interrupt flag sets, the count reads `$FF`, and from then on it goes down by one every pulse, so the count tells how long ago the flag set. Reading with `A0` high gives the flag on `D7`. `A3` on a timer write or a timer read lets the flag pull `PB7` low (1) or not (0): `PB7` is then the processor's interrupt line, and should be an input. Writing the timer, or reading it after the flag's own pulse, clears the flag. (MOS's own data sheet example is one pulse off from this rule.)

`RES_N` low clears both ports' data and direction registers, making every pin an input, and stops the interrupt.

Timing at 1 MHz (maximums, the data sheet gives no typical): a byte read is on `D0`–`D7` 395 ns after `PHI2` rises and released just after it falls; a byte written is taken as `PHI2` falls; port outputs change up to 1 µs after that; `PB7` goes low at least 200 ns before the `PHI2` rise of the flag's pulse.

The inputs are MOS: a pin left floating keeps reading its last value. Its registers, timer and RAM start random at power-on.

## Pins

- `A0`–`A9`: address; `A0` is the lowest bit
- `D0`–`D7`: data bus, driven only while the processor reads this chip
- `PHI2`: the processor's phase-2 clock
- `RW`: high reads, low writes
- `RS0`: ROM select, low selects the ROM
- `CS1`: chip select for the RAM, ports and timer, active low (the pin is `PB6` on other masks)
- `RES_N`: reset, active low
- `PA0`–`PA7`: port A
- `PB0`–`PB5`: port B
- `PB7`: port B, or the timer's interrupt output
- `VCC`: +5 V (pin 20)
- `GND`: 0 V (pin 1)
