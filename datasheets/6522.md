# 6522 — versatile interface adapter (VIA)

## Function

Two 8-bit ports, two timers, a shift register and the interrupts that go with them, as sixteen registers chosen by `RS0`–`RS3` while the chip is selected (`CS1` high, `CS2_N` low) and `PHI2` is high:

- 0 port B, 1 port A (with handshake), 2 port B direction, 3 port A direction;
- 4–7 timer 1: counter low, counter high, latch low, latch high;
- 8, 9 timer 2: counter low, counter high;
- A shift register, B auxiliary control, C peripheral control;
- D interrupt flags, E interrupt enable, F port A without handshake.

A 1 in a direction register makes that line an output. Reading port A gives its pins; reading port B gives the output register on output lines and the pins on inputs. With bit 0 (port A) or bit 1 (port B) of the auxiliary control register set, a read gives instead the levels the pins had at the last active edge of `CA1` (or `CB1`): the port is latched.

**Timer 1** counts down once a cycle from the value written to its high byte (the low byte is written first, to the latch). Written N, it reads N, N−1, … 0, then `$FFFF`; then it sets its interrupt flag and starts again from N, so one round takes N + 2 cycles. Auxiliary control bit 6 makes it run on and interrupt every round; at 0 it interrupts only once per write. With bit 7 set, `PB7` is the timer's own output: low from the write, then in free-running mode turning over at every round (a square wave), or in one-shot mode back high when the time is up. Reading the counter's low byte clears the flag.

**Timer 2** counts the same way once a cycle and interrupts once per write, then keeps counting down past `$FFFF` without starting again. With auxiliary control bit 5 set, it counts the falling edges on `PB6` instead, and interrupts when it reaches 0.

**The shift register** moves eight bits in or out on `CB2`, top bit first, with `CB1` as its clock. Auxiliary control bits 4–2 choose how:

- 0: off;
- 1, 2, 3: shift in, clocked by timer 2, by every second cycle, or by `CB1` from outside;
- 4: shift out for ever, clocked by timer 2 (no interrupt);
- 5, 6, 7: shift out, clocked by timer 2, by every second cycle, or by `CB1` from outside.

Clocked by timer 2, each half of the clock lasts the timer's low latch plus 2 cycles. Reading or writing the register starts eight bits; after them its flag sets.

**The control lines.** `CA1` and `CB1` set their flags on the edge the peripheral control register chooses. `CA2` and `CB2` are inputs, setting their flags on a chosen edge (and in "independent" mode not cleared by reading the port), or outputs:

- handshake: low after the processor uses the port, high on the next active edge of `C1`;
- pulse: low for one cycle after the processor uses the port;
- low, or high.

**Interrupts.** Each flag is a bit of the flag register: 6 timer 1, 5 timer 2, 4 `CB1`, 3 `CB2`, 2 shift register, 1 `CA1`, 0 `CA2`. Bit 7 is 1 while any flag that is also enabled is 1, and then `IRQ_N` is low. Writing a 1 to a flag clears it. Writing the enable register with bit 7 set enables the bits written as 1; with bit 7 clear it disables them. Reading the enable register gives bit 7 as 1 (as the Rockwell data sheet; the MOS sheet says 0).

`RES_N` low clears every register but the two timers' counters and latches and the shift register: every line becomes an input, every interrupt is off.

The port lines, `CA2`, `CB1` and `CB2` have pull-ups inside, so an input left alone reads 1; an output's 1 here is that pull-up's. `IRQ_N` only pulls down. A read's byte is on `D0`–`D7` about 400 ns after `PHI2` rises; port lines change up to 1 µs after the writing cycle; the shift register's `CB2` (and `CB1`, `PB7` here) 300 ns after `PHI2` falls. The handshake lines here change 400 ns after their `PHI2` edge and the interrupt 200 ns after: both assumed. Not modelled: the real chip's lost bits when an outside `CB1` edge falls just as `PHI2` does, and the Rockwell part's shifting in mode 0.

On the PET 2001, the VIA carries the user port on port A, the IEEE-488 and cassette control lines on port B, the screen's retrace on `PB5`, and the choice between the two character sets on `CA2`; programs make sound with its shift register on `CB2`.

## Pins

- `PA0`–`PA7`: port A
- `PB0`–`PB7`: port B; `PB6` counts pulses for timer 2, `PB7` can be timer 1's output
- `CA1`: control input
- `CA2`: control line, input or output
- `CB1`: control line, the shift register's clock
- `CB2`: control line, the shift register's data
- `IRQ_N`: interrupt output, open drain, active low
- `D0`–`D7`: data bus
- `RS0`–`RS3`: register select
- `CS1`: chip select, active high
- `CS2_N`: chip select, active low
- `RW`: high to read, low to write
- `PHI2`: the processor's clock
- `RES_N`: reset, active low
- `VCC`: +5 V
- `GND`: 0 V
