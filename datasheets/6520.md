# 6520 — peripheral interface adapter (PIA)

## Function

Connects the processor to the outside world through two 8-bit ports, A and B, each with four lines of control around it. The processor sees four registers, chosen by `RS1` `RS0` while the chip is selected (`CS0` and `CS1` high, `CS2_N` low) and `PHI2` is high:

- `RS1 RS0` = 00: port A's data, or its direction register when bit 2 of control register A is 0;
- 01: control register A;
- 10: port B's data, or its direction register when bit 2 of control register B is 0;
- 11: control register B.

A 1 in a direction register makes that port line an output, a 0 an input. Reading port A gives the levels on its pins, even on the lines that are outputs. Reading port B gives the output register on the output lines and the pins on the inputs.

Each side has two control lines. `CA1` (`CB1`) is an input: when it changes in the direction bit 1 of its control register chooses (0 falling, 1 rising), bit 7 of the control register goes to 1, and if bit 0 is 1 the side's `IRQ_N` goes low. `CA2` (`CB2`) is an input like it when bit 5 is 0: bit 4 chooses its direction, bit 6 records it, bit 3 lets it interrupt. When bit 5 is 1, it is an output, set by bits 4 and 3:

- 00, handshake: `CA2` goes low after the processor reads port A and back high on the next active edge of `CA1`; `CB2` goes low after the processor writes port B, and back high on the next active edge of `CB1`;
- 01, pulse: the same, but back high one cycle later on its own;
- 10: low; 11: high.

Reading a side's port (not its direction register) clears both of its flags and releases its `IRQ_N`. `RES_N` low clears every register: every line becomes an input, every interrupt is off.

Port A's lines and `CA2` have pull-ups inside the chip, so an input left alone reads 1. Port B's lines and `CB2` float as inputs, and drive strongly as outputs. The `IRQ_N` outputs only pull down, so several chips can share one interrupt line with one resistor.

The chip does everything in step with `PHI2`: a read's byte is on `D0`–`D7` about 400 ns after `PHI2` rises, and output lines change up to 1 µs after the writing cycle ends. The handshake lines here change 400 ns after their `PHI2` edge and the interrupts 200 ns after: these two are assumed (the data sheet gives only maximums for one and no figure for the other).

On the PET 2001, PIA1 scans the keyboard (port A's low four lines choose the row through a 74LS145, port B reads the keys) and takes the screen's retrace on `CB1`, 60 interrupts a second; PIA2 carries the IEEE-488 data.

## Pins

- `PA0`–`PA7`: port A, pull-ups inside
- `PB0`–`PB7`: port B
- `CA1`, `CB1`: control inputs
- `CA2`, `CB2`: control lines, input or output
- `IRQA_N`, `IRQB_N`: interrupt outputs, open drain, active low
- `D0`–`D7`: data bus
- `RS0`, `RS1`: register select
- `CS0`, `CS1`: chip selects, active high
- `CS2_N`: chip select, active low
- `RW`: high to read, low to write
- `PHI2`: the processor's clock
- `RES_N`: reset, active low
- `VCC`: +5 V
- `GND`: 0 V
