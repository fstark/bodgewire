# 2518 — hex 32-bit static shift register

## Function

Six shift registers of 32 bits each, side by side on one clock: six bits at a time, so a 6-bit character code goes in and out as one word. Each register is a ring: on each rise of `CLK` the bit at its output either goes back in at the other end (`REC` high: recirculate) or is replaced by its input (`REC` low: write), and the next bit comes out about 300 ns later. After 32 clocks the ring is back where it started. Used as a line buffer: loaded once with a row of 32 characters, then brought round again and again.

The memory is static: the clock may stop (high) for as long as wanted without losing anything.

The real chip needs +5 V and −12 V (`VGG`); here `VGG` is not connected. Its outputs only pull high, a resistor to −12 V pulls them low; here they drive both ways.

The inputs are MOS: a pin left floating keeps reading its last value.

## Pins

- `IN1`–`IN6`: inputs of the six registers, written while `REC` is low
- `OUT1`–`OUT6`: outputs of the six registers
- `REC`: high to recirculate, low to write the inputs
- `CLK`: clock; the registers move on its rising edge
- `VGG`: −12 V on the real chip, not connected
- `VCC`: +5 V
