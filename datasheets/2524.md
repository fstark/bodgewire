# 2524 — 512-bit dynamic recirculating shift register

## Function

A memory of 512 bits in a ring, with no addresses: one bit at a time comes to the output, and the ring moves one place for each pair of clock pulses. After 512 pairs every bit is back where it started.

The two clocks, `PHI1` and `PHI2`, are pulsed low in turn, never together. As `PHI2` goes back high, the bit at the output is either kept (`WRITE` low: the ring recirculates) or replaced by `IN` (`WRITE` high). As `PHI1` goes back high, the ring moves and the next bit comes out on `OUT`, at most 100 ns later. A bit just written comes out only when it has gone round. `OUT` is 0 while `READ` is low; tie `READ` high to see the bits.

The memory is dynamic: the bits are kept as charges that the clocks move along and refresh. Keep both clocks pulsing: here, if either one stops for more than 10 ms, every bit is lost (it becomes random). The real chip guarantees its bits only above about 1 kHz at room temperature, and keeps them typically 100 times longer.

The real chip needs +5 V and −5 V (`VDD`), and its clocks swing from +5 V down to −12 V, made by a clock driver. On these Boards the clocks come straight from TTL gates and `VDD` is not connected. The real output only pulls high, a resistor on the board pulls it low; here `OUT` drives both ways.

The inputs are MOS: a pin left floating keeps reading its last value.

## Pins

- `PHI1`: output clock, pulsed low; the ring moves as it goes back high
- `PHI2`: input clock, pulsed low; the bit at the output is kept or written as it goes back high
- `IN`: the bit written while `WRITE` is high
- `WRITE`: high to write `IN`, low to recirculate
- `READ`: high to show the bits on `OUT`, low for 0
- `OUT`: the bit at the output
- `VDD`: −5 V on the real chip, not connected
- `VCC`: +5 V
