# 130 — BW-2001

The first computer with a screen: the Commodore PET 2001's main board (board 320137), chip for chip and wire for wire, running Commodore's own ROMs, BASIC 1 (decided 2026-11-22; until then our firmware). Our firmware (P5p: Tiny BASIC, a full-screen editor, a monitor and a self-test) stays as the debug ROMs, `debug-roms/`, run with `--roms machines/130-bw2001/debug-roms`; the tests that drive the firmware use it. Its picture is made by TTL counters reading the video RAM 60 times a second, with no help from the 6502, and the 6502 shares that RAM with them. Chips came with P5n ([BW2001_CHIPS.md](../../../docs/p5n-bw2001-chips/BW2001_CHIPS.md)), the ROMs with P5p ([BW2001_FIRMWARE.md](../../../docs/p5p-bw2001-firmware/BW2001_FIRMWARE.md)), the Machine with P5q ([BW2001_MACHINE.md](../../../docs/p5q-bw2001/BW2001_MACHINE.md)).

## Lesson

The picture is a second machine on the same board. An 8 MHz clock, divided down, counts dots, characters, scan lines and rows; the count is an address in the video RAM; the byte there and the scan line are an address in the character ROM; a shift register sends its dots to the monitor. The 6502 runs off the same divided clock and reaches the same video RAM through multiplexers that hand it the RAM's address lines whenever it reads or writes the screen. The video, which never waits, then draws whatever byte the 6502 is touching: snow. The PET's editor waits for the retrace before each screen store to avoid it, and a famous `POKE` that stops that wait makes the screen fast and snowy.

So a fault on this board shows either in the picture (the video machine: a missing line of dots, a doubled column, a rolling frame, a screen of `@`) or in what the 6502 does (the computer: no banner, a wrong byte count, a dead key), and the two share the video RAM, the clock and the power.

## Circuit

From Commodore's schematic 320135 sheets 1–3 for board 320137 (zimmers.net), transcribed pin by pin in [schematic/](schematic/) and checked by running Commodore's own BASIC 1 on it (P5q). Locations are the board's grid: a letter (row A at the back, J at the front) and a number (column 1 at the left, 9 at the right): `UF3` is the 6502 in row F, column 3.

- **Clock** (sheet 3): `X1`, an 8 MHz `OSC` in place of the crystal and its two 74LS04 gates, through `UE2` (a gate a test point can hold low) to `CLK8`. `UC9` 74LS93 divides it to 2 MHz and 1 MHz; `UC8` re-times the 1 MHz into `PHI0` for the 6502, one dot later.
- **CPU and buses** (sheet 1): `UF3` 6502. `A1`–`A10` through two 7417s (`UE3`, `UE4`, open collector, 1K pull-ups `R28`–`R38`) to the buffered `BA` bus; `A0` through a gate of `UE2`, `A11` through two inverters of `UE5`. `UG5`, `UG6` 74LS244 pairs join `D0`–`D7` (CPU, PIAs, VIA) to the buffered `BD` bus (RAM, video RAM, ROMs, expansion): `UG4` 74LS21 turns them towards the CPU for a read outside `$C000`–`$FFFF`.
- **Decoding**: `UG2` 74LS154 makes one select per 4 KB block, `SEL0_N`–`SELF_N`. `SEL8_N`, inverted by `UE5` to `SEL8`, is the screen. `SELE_N` with `BA11` and `BA4`/`BA5`/`BA6` selects PIA1 `$E810`, PIA2 `$E820` and the VIA `$E840` (all three mirrored in `$E800`–`$EFFF`).
- **RAM** (sheet 2): sixteen 2114s of the 300 ns grade (the 2114-3: the video path misses with the 450 ns one), 8 KB (`UI1`–`UI8` the low nibble, `UJ1`–`UJ8` the high), on their own data bus `BBD0`–`BBD7` behind `UH8`, `UH9` 74LS244s, each wired half one way and half the other; `UI9` 74LS139 decodes the 1 KB blocks from `SEL0_N`, `SEL1_N`, `BA10`, `BA11`; `UJ9` makes `RW_RAM`, low in the second half of a write.
- **ROM**: seven 2316B sockets `UH1`–`UH7`, `$C000`–`$DFFF` and `$E000`–`$FFFF`: Commodore's BASIC 1, 901447-01 to -07 (`roms/`, tests/data/pet_roms.md): BASIC in `UH1`, `UH5`, `UH2`, `UH6`, the editor in `UH3`, the operating system in `UH4` and `UH7` (reset at `$FD38`). Any socket can be filled from another directory at run time (`--roms DIR`, P5q2): `debug-roms/` holds our firmware, a file per socket cut by `debug-roms/build.sh` from the images assembled in `debug-roms/src/` (BASIC in `UH1`/`UH5`, the editor in `UH3`, the monitor in `UH4`, reset and the self-test in `UH7`, `UH2` and `UH6` empty).
- **Video** (sheet 3): the character counter (`UC9`, `UB5`, `UC7`: 64 characters of 1 µs a line), display enable `DIS_ON` and `HORZ_DRIVE` (`UC5`), the scan-line counter `UA1`, the vertical state `UB6` (vertical drive, top blank, video on, bottom blank: 20 + 20 + 200 + 20 = 260 lines, 60.1 Hz), the video address counters `UD5`, `UD6`, `UD7` (74177s) reloaded from the row-start latch `UE6` (74100) for each of the 8 scan lines of a row; `UD2`–`UD4` 74LS157s choose the video address or the CPU's `BA0`–`BA9` (`SEL8`); the video RAM `UC3`, `UC4` (2114s, 1 KB, 40 × 25 used) on `SD0`–`SD7`, joined to `BD` by `UB3`, `UB4` 74LS244s for the CPU; the character ROM `UA2` (2316B, 901447-08), `UB2` 74LS165 shifting dots out at 8 MHz, `UC1` 74LS74 carrying the reverse-video bit in step; `UE9` gates the dot with `VIDEO_ON`, `DIS_ON` and `BLANK_TV` onto `TV_VIDEO`, low for a lit dot.
- **I/O** (sheet 1): `UG8` 6520 PIA1 (keyboard rows on `PA0`–`PA3` through `UG9` 74LS145, columns on `PB0`–`PB7` with 10K pull-ups `R18`–`R25`, the cassette switches, `EOI` in, DIAG on `PA7`, `BLANK_TV`/`EOI` out on `CA2`, the retrace `VIDEO_ON` on `CB1`); `UB8` 6520 PIA2 (IEEE-488 data in and out); `UA5` 6522 VIA (user port `PA0`–`PA7`, `CA1`, `CB2`; IEEE handshake; cassette write and motor; the retrace on `PB5`; the character set on `CA2`, `GRAPHIC`). `UA7`–`UA9` MC3446 bus transceivers between PIA2/VIA and the IEEE connector.
- **Reset**: `UDE9`, a `POR` in place of the PET's 555 (which holds reset for about a second), on `RES_N`; `R99` its pull-up.
- **Diagnostic LED** `DS1`: lit while software selects keyboard row 11 (`UE9`, `UD9`).
- **Connectors**: `J1` IEEE-488, `J2` user port (with the DIAG pin and the video signals), `J3` and `J6` cassette #2 and #1, `J5` the keyboard (behind it the BW-2001 keyboard, a floating window), `J7` the monitor, `J8` power. The PET's `J4` memory expansion is left out.

Left out, as on the BW-1: the power supply, the cassette motor transistors (their outputs come straight from the VIA and PIA pins to the cassette headers) and the IEEE bus terminations' pull-downs. The PET 2001's own monitor is our TV, on separate drives as the PET's built-in one.

## When it works

- The TV locks at once: 260 lines, a 40 × 25 window of text in the middle, black border.
- Right after power-on the screen shows the video RAM's random power-on contents, until the reset code has sized the RAM and clears it; then the banner, by 0.6 s:

```
*** COMMODORE BASIC ***

 7167 BYTES FREE

READY.
```

  with a blinking cursor below (the blink: the retrace interrupt reads and rewrites `$80C8` about three times a second).
- Typed keys echo; a program runs. The editor writes each character only while the beam is outside the text (it waits for the VIA's `PB5`), and blanks the whole picture while it scrolls (PIA1 `CA2`, `BLANK_TV`), as on the PET 2001.
- No snow while BASIC prints. `POKE 59458,62` (the PET's "killer poke", which turns the VIA's retrace input into an output) makes printing faster and puts brief white specks over the text while it prints; scrolling stays blanked; the keyboard still works.
- `DS1` dark (no software selects row 11).
- With the debug ROMs: the banner `*** BW-2001 TINY BASIC ***`, `7166 BYTES FREE`, `READY`; `PRINT USR(61440)` enters the monitor (`C*`); `.T` runs the self-test; so does reset with a Bodge Wire from `J2` pin 5 (DIAG) to a `J2` ground pin. Their editor scrolls without blanking, about 0.15 s a row.

Instruments: `CLK8` 8 MHz, `PHI0` 1 MHz; `HORZ_DRIVE` 15.625 kHz, high 24 µs; `VERT_DRIVE` low 1.28 ms every 16.64 ms; `SEL8` high only when the 6502 touches the screen; `IRQ_N` pulled low once a frame (the retrace interrupt).

## Speed

The 8 MHz dot clock and everything it drives make this the heaviest Machine: 0.23× real time headless (release), 0.22× in `bw_ui` on the Board with every instrument; the video alone runs at 0.49×. The 8 MHz clock and its gated copy are 28 % of all Net changes. Faster kernels are their own roadmap row (P5z, at the end of P5).

## Puzzles

Seventeen Puzzles (P5r; Slanted banner from P8k, 2026-11-28), in four kinds, written on our firmware and moved to Commodore's BASIC 1 on 2026-11-22: each shows on BASIC 1's own screens. The debug ROMs' self-test (Service Manual) still names the memory faults.

The picture:

- Odd E: every `E` has a stray dot inside it, every other letter right: one cell of the character ROM `UA2`, not the video RAM (which holds the right codes).
- Striped screen: a vertical line through every character position, blank ones too: `CD3` cracked between the character ROM and the shift register `UB2`, whose input floats high.
- Rolling text: the picture drifts and never locks: `VERT_DRIVE` cracked just before the monitor connector `J7`.
- Stutter: every character twice, every other one lost (`**  OOMMDDRR  AAII…`): the multiplexer `UD2`'s `SA0` stuck low, for the video and the 6502 alike.
- Negative: the text window a bright block, the letters black: `UC1`'s `REV_D` stuck high.

The computer, seen in use:

- Short memory: `2047 BYTES FREE`: bit 0 of `UI5` ($0C00) stuck high stops BASIC 1's RAM sizing there. (Stuck low, as on our firmware until 2026-11-22, the bit passes BASIC 1's test values: the fault hid.)
- Missing keys: Q, W, A, S, Z, X, `!`, `"`, RVS and the left SHIFT dead, the keyboard's column 0: `KIN0` cracked between `J5` and PIA1, whose pull-up keeps it high.
- Frozen cursor: the banner, then a cursor that never blinks and keys that do nothing: PIA1 `UG8` deaf to `CB1`, the retrace interrupt that scans the keys and blinks the cursor.
- Random screen: the power-on characters forever: `UDE9`'s `RES_N` stuck low.
- Snow: specks over the text whenever it prints, as after the killer poke (scrolling stays blanked): the VIA `UA5` reads its `PB5` input (the beam outside the text) as always low, though `VIDEO_ON` toggles at its leg, so the editor never waits. Swap the VIA.

Memory, named by the debug ROMs' self-test:

- Fizzing jumble (`11-bad-zero-page`, titled Bad zero page until 2026-11-22: the title named the cause): never a banner; the power-on characters stay, under snow, as BASIC 1's start-up runs wild through memory, the screen's among it. Bit 0 of `UI8` stuck high (self-test `ZERO PAGE BAD 0001 01`). The standard RAM Puzzle: swap it.
- Fizzing jumble again (`12-wrong-chip`, titled Wrong chip until 2026-11-22): the same screen, but swapping `UI8` repairs nothing: a dry Joint on `UH8` pin 17, the RAM buffer's read input for bit 0 (leg and pad differ).
- Ghost letter: the banner reads `COMMODORG BASIC`: one cell of the video RAM `UC3` (self-test `VIDEO RAM BAD 800C 02`).
- Garbled text: every character with an even code one along (`CASIC`), the empty screen full of `!`: bit 0 of the video RAM `UC3` stuck high in every cell. Swap `UC3`.

Dead, found with the instruments:

- Dark monitor (`15-no-clock`, titled No clock until 2026-11-22): no picture, the monitor unlocked: `OSC8` cracked between `X1` and `UE2`; `CLK8` still.
- Frozen jumble (`16-joined-bits`, titled Joined bits until 2026-11-22): the power-on characters forever, as Random screen, but `RES_N` is high: `D0` and `D1` bridged on the 6502.
- Slanted banner: the monitor dark but for slanting streaks of the boot text near the top: the can `X1` runs at 7.6 MHz though marked 8 MHz (a Wrong Clock), every line 5 % long, past the monitor's hold. The scope on `OSC8` reads 7.6 MHz.
