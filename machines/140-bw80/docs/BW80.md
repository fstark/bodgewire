# 140 — BW-80

The Sinclair ZX80, chip for chip: a Z80 and twenty standard TTL and memory chips, no custom part, running the ZX80's own 4K ROM. There is nothing on the screen unless the CPU, the ROM, the RAM and the whole display chain work, because the CPU makes the picture itself, sync included. Chips came with P5u ([BW80_CHIPS.md](../../../docs/p5u-bw80-chips/BW80_CHIPS.md)), the picture with P5v ([BW80_VIDEO.md](../../../docs/p5v-bw80-video/BW80_VIDEO.md)), the Machine with P5x ([BW80_MACHINE.md](../../../docs/p5x-bw80/BW80_MACHINE.md)).

## Lesson

A computer can draw its own picture with no video chip at all. The ZX80's ROM "executes" the display file: the CPU fetches each character code from RAM, eight open-collector gates force a `NOP` onto its side of the data bus while a latch keeps the code, and in the refresh cycle that follows, the character ROM is addressed by that code and a line counter instead of by the CPU. A shift register sends the glyph's dots out at 6.5 MHz. The `HALT` at the end of each row stops the CPU until the refresh counter's `A6` interrupts it, and the interrupt's acknowledge starts the horizontal sync. An `IN` from the keyboard holds the sync down for the vertical pulse. So the computer and the picture are one machine: a fault on the data bus, the ROM, the RAM, the address multiplexers or the sync latches shows on the screen, and the screen goes blank whenever the CPU has something else to do, as it did on every ZX80.

The keyboard is the same economy: no decoder, no port chip. The address lines `A8`–`A15` select the eight rows during an `IN` instruction, through a diode each, and five pull-ups and a buffer put the columns on `D0`–`D4`.

## Circuit

From Grant Searle's redrawn ZX80 issue 2 schematic ([BW80_VIDEO_DESIGN.md](../../../docs/p5v-bw80-video/BW80_VIDEO_DESIGN.md) §1 for the display in detail), every chip at Sinclair's designator:

- **Clock**: `X1`, a 6.5 MHz `OSC` standing for the ceramic resonator and IC20's two gates; IC18 halves it to 3.25 MHz, `PHI`; two paralleled gates of IC15 (open collector, `R20`) buffer it into the Z80's `CLK`. `PHI_N` enables the data latch; the 6.5 MHz `DOT2` clocks the dots.
- **CPU**: IC1, the Z80A. `INT_N` is `A6` (the refresh counter's bit 6). `NMI_N`, `BUSRQ_N`, `WAIT_N` pulled up (`R12`, `R22`, `R23`); `RST1`, a `POR`, on `RESET_N` with `R21`.
- **Memory**: IC2, the 4K ROM (`2332`, `machines/140-bw80/roms/zx80_ic2.bin`, Sinclair's; the character set at $0E00), selected with `A14` low and `A12` low (`CS2`), mirrored at $2000, $8000, $A000. IC3 and IC4, two 2114s, 1K at $4000, mirrored through $7FFF and above $C000. IC12 makes `RAM_SEL_N` from `MREQ` and `A14`, through `R19` (so an expansion can override it) to the RAMs' `CS_N`; `ROM_SEL_N` from `MREQ` and `A14` low, held through the refresh by IC6.
- **The two data buses**: the CPU's `D0`–`D7` and the memories' `MD0`–`MD7` joined by 1K resistors `R4`–`R11`. The eight open-collector gates (IC14, two of IC15) pull `D` low when `NOP_EN` is high: an `M1` fetch with `A15` high, the CPU not halted, and the fetched code's bit 6 low (`MD6` through IC13 and IC16, IC17). `R28` pulls `MD6` up so a keyboard read gives bit 6 high (the UK margin: the ROM counts 56 blank lines above the picture).
- **The character chain**: IC5 (`74LS373`) latches the code while `MREQ` is low with `RFSH_N` high and `PHI_N` high, its bit 6's place holding the NOP condition (`NOP_COND_N` into pin 8, out as `CHAR_FETCH_N`: low after a character fetch); IC6–IC8 (`74LS157`) give the ROM the CPU's `A0`–`A8` or, in the refresh, the latched code `CH0`–`CH5` and the line counter `LC0`–`LC2`; IC9 (`74LS165`) loads the glyph row at the next rise of `PHI` (the pulse from `C11`/`R25`, gated by IC16 with `CHAR_FETCH` and `MREQ_N`: only after a character fetch, never while the ROM's own code runs or the CPU is halted) and shifts it out at each rise of `DOT2` (`C9`/`R2`); `QH_N` through IC20's XOR with the inverse latch (IC12, set by a code's bit 7 at the load, reset at the next load pulse) to `VIDEO`. IC21 (`74LS93`) counts the sync pulses into `LC0`–`LC2`, cleared through the vertical sync.
- **Sync**: IC17 decodes `IORD_N` and `IOWR_N`; `KBD_N` is an `IN` from an even port. IC11's NAND latch: a keyboard read takes `VS_N` low (the vertical sync: `SYNC` held low through IC19's clear, the line counter reset), an `OUT` sets it again. Each `IORQ_N` (the interrupt acknowledge above all) presets IC18's latch; IC19 counts two rises of `M1_CLK` to take `SYNC` low and two more to raise it: the horizontal sync, 20 T-states with the ROM's interrupt routine.
- **Keyboard**: IC10 (`74LS365`) puts `KB0`–`KB4` and `EAR` on `D0`–`D4` and `D7` while `KBD_N` is low. `R13`–`R17` (47k) pull the columns up; `R1` pulls `EAR` down (no recorder). `J3` is the keyboard: the BW-80's membrane keyboard in its own window, the diodes `D3`–`D10` inside it, reading `A8`–`A15` and pulling a held key's column low.
- **Connectors**: `J1` power, `J2` video (`VIDEO` and `SYNC` to the TV, where the ZX80's modulator mixes them), `J3` the keyboard, `J4` the expansion bus (the ZX80's 2 × 23 edge connector: the memory-side data bus, `RAM_CS_N`, the address bus, the Z80's control lines, `PHI_N`; nothing plugged in).

Left out: the cassette (`MIC`, the `EAR` network), the modulator and the 7805 regulator, the US-only diode `D11`. Stand-ins: `X1` for the resonator, `RST1` for `R21`/`C10` (1 ms in place of about 0.2 s).

## When it works

- Power on: the ROM clears its 1K and builds an empty display file (about 0.2 s), then the TV locks on a 24-row picture with the inverse `K` cursor at the bottom left: the editor, waiting for a command.
- The screen blanks whenever the ROM is busy: during a key's processing and while a program runs. This is the ZX80, not a fault.
- A letter key types its keyword when the cursor is `K` (O: PRINT, at the start of a line or after THEN), the letter when it is `L`. NEW LINE enters the line; `PRINT 2+2` shows `4` at the top and the report `0/-2` at the bottom (report code 0; line -2 is the edit line, where a direct command runs). After `RUN`, the report names the last line: `0/20`. A line with a number is stored; `RUN` runs the program; `LIST` lists it.
- The keyboard window: click a key, or type (the PC keys always go to the Machine, P6o): each PC key presses the ZX80 key with the same character, SHIFT added where the ZX80 needs it (`"` is SHIFT+Y). Enter is NEW LINE, Backspace RUBOUT, the arrows the cursor keys, Home HOME, Escape BREAK, Shift+Enter EDIT, Shift with a letter SHIFT with that key (the graphics, OR, `**`). Cmd+V (Ctrl+V) pastes a listing: keywords typed as their keys, so a program pasted from a book runs.
- `SAVE` writes nothing and `LOAD` waits for ever (BREAK stops it): there is no recorder.

Instruments: `DOT` 6.5 MHz, `CLK` 3.25 MHz; `SYNC` low 6.15 µs every 64 µs, held low about 400 µs once a frame (50 Hz); `NOP_EN` high during the 32 character fetches of a row; `KBD_N` low once a frame while waiting for a key; `A15` high through each display row.

## Speed

0.25× real time headless on the ZX80's ROM (P5v, unchanged by the keyboard: it drives nothing until a key is held), 0.23× in `bw_ui` on the Board with every instrument attached (UI 1.1 ms): the resistor networks, the two pulse shapers and the wide decoding cost events on every fetch. At that speed a key typed on the PC is queued in simulation time, so none is lost.

## Puzzles

Sixteen Puzzles (P5y; Stray cursor from P8k, 2026-11-28), on the ZX80's ROM, in four kinds. The CPU draws the picture itself, so a fault in the display chain shows in the picture while the computer works on behind it, and a fault on its buses leaves the TV with nothing to lock on.

The picture (the TV locks, the display file in RAM is right):

- Q everywhere: the empty places on the screen fill with letters, `Q`s mostly, the margins full of junk, the typed text readable over it: `IC16`'s load gate stuck open, so every memory access loads a glyph: each row's `HALT` (a `Q`), then the byte the halted CPU fetches after it, to the row's end.
- Doubled lines: squat, striped letters made of their even rows, each twice: the line counter `IC21`'s bit 0 stuck low.
- Bottom halves: the top half of every letter is its bottom half: `LC2` cracked between the line counter and the multiplexer `IC8`, whose input floats high.
- Wrong letters: `PRINT` reads `LRILT`, `HELLO` `DCLLK`; the results are right: `CH1` and `CH2` bridged on the data latch `IC5`, both reading low where they differ.
- Quotes for spaces: every space a `"`, `0` as `1`, `E` as `F`, `O` as `P`, the cursor an `L`: a dry Joint on `IC5` pin 6, the latch's bit 0, which `IC8` reads high.
- Plain cursor: the cursor a plain `K`, not white on black: the inverse latch `IC12` stuck low.
- Blank page: a white page with one black square where the cursor is, typing changing nothing visible: the shift register `IC9`'s clock input stuck.

The keyboard, seen in use:

- Eight keys: X, D, E, 3, 8, I, K and M never type: `KB2` cracked at the keyboard buffer `IC10`, whose input floats high.
- The same eight keys: the same keys dead, but a mend finds nothing: `IC10`'s input stuck high inside; the leg reads the key.
- No PRINT: P, O, I, U and Y do nothing (no PRINT, INPUT, IF, REM, no quote mark): the keyboard's row `A13` cut off at `J3`.

The sync:

- Rolling screen: the picture rolls and never settles, the keys still working: `KBD_N` cracked just before the vertical sync latch, which is never reset.
- Stray cursor: a white screen with a dark band along the bottom, the K cursor alone in its middle, never locked: the can `X1` runs at 3.25 MHz though marked 6.5 MHz (a Wrong Clock), so the CPU, which draws the picture, runs at half speed. The scope on `DOT` reads 3.25 MHz.
- Torn picture: slanting streaks, never locked: the sync latch `IC18` stuck, no horizontal pulse ever armed.

Dead, found with the instruments:

- Still starting: the screen stays blank and unlocked after power-on, the keys dead: bit 2 of every RAM byte stuck low in `IC3`, so the ROM's clear-and-check never ends. Swap `IC3`.
- Vertical stripes: fine vertical stripes, never locked: `ROM_CS1` cracked between `IC6` and the ROM, which is never selected; the select moves at `IC6` pin 7 and sits high at `IC2` pin 20.
- White screen: white from power-on, never locked: a NOP gate of `IC14` stuck low holds `D3` low on the CPU's side of the bus while `CLK` runs.
