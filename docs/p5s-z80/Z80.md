# P5s — Z80 Part

Roadmap step P5s ([ARCHITECTURE.md](../ARCHITECTURE.md#roadmap)): the NMOS Z80 as a catalogue Part, checked against public conformance data, and measured on the P5a bus load with a real program in place of the stub. Design: [Z80_DESIGN.md](Z80_DESIGN.md). Plan: [Z80_PLAN.md](Z80_PLAN.md). Terminology: [GLOSSARY.md](../GLOSSARY.md).

## Why

The BW-80 (P5u–P5y) is a ZX80, and the ZX80 makes its picture out of the Z80's own bus cycles: M1 fetches whose data the hardware replaces with NOPs, the refresh address carrying the character code to the ROM, `HALT` ending each line, the interrupt acknowledge starting HSYNC, `IN` and `OUT` driving VSYNC (ARCHITECTURE, Component models). A Z80 that is right per instruction but wrong per T-state gives a broken picture far from its cause. So, as with the 6502 (P5c), the instruction core is written apart from the Pins and checked against public test suites before it drives a circuit, and the Part around it is checked edge by edge against the data sheet.

P5a measured the bus load of a ZX80-shaped circuit with a stub that plays a fixed frame; P5b made the kernel faster against it. This step runs the same circuit with a real CPU and a real program, so the BW-80's speed is known before its chips are built.

## User decisions (2026-11-15)

| Question | Decision |
|---|---|
| The bus load | a new netlist `netlists/bus_load_z80.bwn` beside `bus_load.bwn`: the real Z80 in place of `Z80STUB`, `INT_N` on `A6` as on the ZX80, a power-on reset on `RESET_N`, and a ROM program written by a Python script as commented bytes (no Z80 assembler before P5t) that builds the stub's page. `bus_load.bwn` and `Z80STUB` stay as they are, so P5a/P5b's figures and `bench/trace_check.sh` stay comparable. The same TV-page test runs on both; both ratios are recorded |
| Variant | Zilog NMOS, where variants differ (the X/Y flags of `SCF`/`CCF` through Q, `OUT (C),0`): the variant all the test data describes. The ZX80's NEC µPD780C is not copied |
| Test data | as the 6502: a subset of Tom Harte's Z80 SingleStepTests (25 cases of each file's 1,000, MIT, licence kept) committed and run in every ctest; a script fetches the full Harte set and ZEXDOC/ZEXALL (GPL, never committed) into git-ignored `external/z80/`; their cases `SKIP` without it, run only in release (hidden tag), once in this step, the result recorded |
| Pins | all exact: `WAIT_N` sampled on T2's and each Tw's falling edge; `BUSRQ_N` at the last T-state of each M-cycle, address, data and the memory and I/O strobes floating while `BUSAK_N` is low; `NMI_N` on its edge, `INT_N` on its level; IM 0 executes whatever the bus gives |

Decided while designing (2026-11-15), for the user to overrule:

- Part name `Z80`, class `Z80`, core `Z80Core`; the Zilog pinout on DIP-40 (as `Z80STUB`: 1–5 `A11`–`A15`, 6 `CLK`, 7 `D4`, 8 `D3`, 9 `D5`, 10 `D6`, 11 `VCC`, 12 `D2`, 13 `D7`, 14 `D0`, 15 `D1`, 16 `INT_N`, 17 `NMI_N`, 18 `HALT_N`, 19 `MREQ_N`, 20 `IORQ_N`, 21 `RD_N`, 22 `WR_N`, 23 `BUSAK_N`, 24 `WAIT_N`, 25 `BUSRQ_N`, 26 `RESET_N`, 27 `M1_N`, 28 `RFSH_N`, 29 `GND`, 30–40 `A0`–`A10`).
- The pins are NMOS: `Family::Mos` (a floating input keeps its last level, X reads as a random bit), as the 6502.
- Pin timing: the Z80A (4 MHz) data sheet's figures, maxima where it gives no typical (the stub's `kZ80…` delays, plus the few it lacked).
- Power-on: every register random and the CPU at an opcode fetch at a random PC, as the 6502. A real NMOS Z80 needs `RESET_N` held low for three clocks; ours runs from wherever it lands until reset. `bus_load_z80.bwn` has a `POR`.
- Reset (Zilog, and Sean Young's "The Undocumented Z80 Documented", §2.4): PC, I, R = 0, IFF1 = IFF2 = 0, IM 0, AF = SP = `$FFFF`; while `RESET_N` is low the address and data pins float and the control outputs are high.
- No split: core, Part, data and the bus load fit one step of six phases. What Harte's data does not cover (interrupts, IM 0, HALT's exit, the prefix chains `DD DD`/`DD FD`/`DD ED`, the `ED` holes outside `40`–`7F`) gets hand-written cases.

## What is new

1. **Instruction core** (`Z80Core`). Pure C++, no simulator types, one machine cycle (M-cycle) at a time: `bus()` gives the M-cycle in progress (kind, address, data written, its T-states, the internal T-states after it); `step(data)` ends it with the byte read. It covers:
   - every opcode of the five tables (unprefixed, `CB`, `ED`, `DD`/`FD`, `DD CB`/`FD CB`), documented or not, with each M-cycle's kind, address and length as on the chip: the 5- and 6-T-state M1s, the internal T-states of `JR`, `DJNZ`, `EX (SP),HL`, block instructions and indexed addressing, the repeat of `LDIR` and its family (PC back by 2, five more T-states);
   - the undocumented behaviour Harte's data records: the X and Y flags (bits 3 and 5) everywhere, MEMPTR (WZ) and its leak into `BIT n,(HL)`, Q and its effect on `SCF`/`CCF`, the flags of repeated block instructions, `DD`/`FD` before an opcode that has no index form (a 4-T-state prefix, then the opcode), `DD CB d op` copying the result into a register, `SLL`, `IN F,(C)`/`IN (C)`, `OUT (C),0`, the `ED` mirrors (`NEG`, `RETN`, `IM`) and the `ED` holes as 8-T-state NOPs;
   - R: bits 0–6 counted per M1 (two for a prefixed opcode), bit 7 kept as loaded; the refresh address `I:R` on each M1;
   - interrupts: `INT` sampled at the last T-state of each instruction when IFF1 is set and the last instruction was not `EI` or a prefix; IM 0 (the opcode read in the acknowledge cycle executed, with its operand bytes read from the bus), IM 1 (`RST 38h`), IM 2 (the vector `I:byte`); NMI latched on its falling edge, taken before INT, IFF1 cleared and IFF2 kept, `RETN` restoring it; the NMOS `LD A,I`/`LD A,R` P/V lost when an interrupt follows;
   - `HALT`: fetches at the address after it (opcode ignored, NOP M1s with refresh, PC kept) until an interrupt or reset;
   - reset: the reset values, then a fetch at `$0000`.
2. **`Z80` Part** (`Z80`): the core behind the Pins, woken only by `CLK`'s edges (both); every other input `sampled`. A T-state sequencer drives the pins of each M-cycle per the data sheet's timing diagrams, inserts wait states while `WAIT_N` is low at T2's (or Tw's) falling edge, samples `INT_N` and `BUSRQ_N` on each cycle's last rising edge and `NMI_N` on every one, floats the bus while `BUSRQ_N` is granted (the core waits, unaware), and holds everything in reset while `RESET_N` is low (A and D floating, the strobes high). `datasheets/Z80.md`.
3. **Test data and tools.** `tests/data/z80/`: the committed Harte subset in a compact text format (as `tests/data/6502/`), the converter, `fetch.sh` for the full Harte set and ZEXDOC/ZEXALL into `external/z80/`. A CP/M stub in the test (`BDOS` calls 2 and 9 at `$0005`, the end at `$0000`) runs the `.com` files on the core.
4. **The bus load with a real CPU.** `netlists/bus_load_z80.bwn` (P5a's circuit, the Z80 in U2, `INT_N` on `A6`, a `POR` U27 with a pull-up on `RESET_N`), `roms/bus_load_z80.rom` written by `roms/make_bus_load_z80_rom.py`: the P5a glyphs at `$0600`, the page, and a ZX80-style display program: copy the page into RAM, then frames of VSYNC (`IN`/`OUT` `FE`), top border, 24 rows × 8 lines executed from the display file's mirror at `$C000` (characters forced to NOPs, `HALT` at each NEWLINE, the interrupt from R bit 6 on `A6`), bottom border; 312 lines, 207 T-states each where the stub had them.

## Tests

- **Core, hand-written:** a NOP's M1 and refresh; R counting with prefixes and bit 7 kept; reset values; `HALT`'s fetches and its exit; `EI` delay; IM 0 (`RST`, and a `CALL` with its operands from the bus), IM 1, IM 2 (vector, pushed PC); NMI on its edge once, over INT, `RETN`; INT not taken between a prefix and its opcode, taken during `LDIR` between repeats with PC at the instruction; the `LD A,I` P/V loss; the prefix chains and `ED` holes Harte lacks; a short NMI pulse; each checked to fail on a mutation of its rule.
- **Core, Harte subset:** the 1,604 files, 25 cases each: every T-state's address, data and read/write/memory/I-O flags (Harte's "simplified" sampling), the ports, then the registers (WZ, Q, P, IFF1/2, IM, `ei` included) and the RAM.
- **Core, full suites (`external/z80/`, release, hidden tag):** all 1.6 M Harte cases; ZEXDOC and ZEXALL to their end with no `ERROR` in their output.
- **Part:** catalogue row, pins, power and package; per `CLK` edge, the pins of an M1, a memory read, a write, an `IN`, an `OUT` and an interrupt acknowledge against `delays::`; the data read before the sampling edge; `WAIT_N` adding Tw on a memory cycle and on an I/O cycle; `BUSRQ_N` floating the bus and `BUSAK_N` low, then the CPU going on; `RESET_N` through the pins (floating, values, the first fetch at `$0000`); `INT_N` and `NMI_N` through the pins; `HALT_N`; X on the data bus read as a random bit; no evaluation from a non-clock input alone.
- **Bus load:** `bus_load_z80.bwn` loads without warnings; the TV shows the page (the `bus_load` test, two seeds); its line and frame counts as the stub's.
- **Release:** `bw run` ratio of `bus_load_z80.bwn` against `bus_load.bwn` (best of 3), `bw_ui` screenshot viewed; the full Harte set, ZEXDOC and ZEXALL run once, their times recorded.

## Later

- The NEC µPD780C's flags (a BW-80 Puzzle could never see them).
- Internal Faults (a stuck register bit, a dead interrupt line): a Puzzle row.
- The NMOS minimum clock (dynamic registers): not modelled; a stopped `CLK` freezes the CPU.
- Kernel work if the ratio calls for it: P5b's open options (ARCHITECTURE, Performance), decided with the user on the figures.
